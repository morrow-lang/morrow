* **Status**: Adopted; major index regression corrected, short two-scheduler residual and strict parity remain open
* **Decision**: Count candidate root/payload words and foreign allocation records with bounded metadata work before transfer validation. Use direct per-heap interval lookup when its estimated probe count is smaller; otherwise build the foreign-allocation index introduced in Decision162. Both paths reject the same known foreign base/interior edges before any ownership mutation, and all metadata/probe work consumes the existing bound. This supersedes the unconditional-index choice, without changing transport admission or ownership.
* **Context**: The first parity checkpoint improved pinned request/reply and CPU contention, but an interleaved 144-process comparison proved a two-scheduler stealing regression. Its preserved [profile](../benchmarks/language-comparison/results/actors-parity-checkpoint1-rr-stealing-profile-20260919/STATUS.md) places 50.6% of non-condition-wait samples in heap detachment. Precise collection makes the candidate small while unrelated heaps can retain thousands of blocks, making eager index construction disproportionately expensive.
* **Independent evidence**: A three-word candidate with 4,096 unrelated allocations must validate within 32 work units. The unconditional index fails with WorkLimit; adaptive validation succeeds. Separate direct-path tests reject foreign root and payload interior addresses and exhaust an eight-unit budget without losing the source heap or roots. The earlier 256-heap/5,000-word index oracle remains unchanged and passes. Thirteen focused transfer tests and all-target runtime Clippy pass. ThreadSanitizer passes 191 tests in 11.82 s, with the two long lifetime-churn tests reserved for the ordinary gate.
* **Acceptance**: The full macOS ARM64 gate passes 2,382 Rust tests across 292 result suites (one nested subprocess result is interleaved in the log), 317 native fixtures, 20 examples, 63 dynamic compatibility programs, 295 atomic rejections and 64+192+231 fuzz cases. Default actor replay retains trace `0xd4e402a412f11e2f`, 48,739 callbacks and zero cleanup residue. The ordinary runtime suite contains 193 tests.
* **Measured correction**: Two new quiet Rust-runner comparisons retain 288 successful processes and all raw data. Against the unconditional index, stealing request/reply improves 1.580×/1.992× at two schedulers and 1.289×/1.339× at four for the short/long workloads. Against the original Decision161 baseline, pinned two/four-scheduler throughput improves 1.21–1.45× and long stealing improves 1.062×/1.307×. Short two-scheduler stealing remains 0.925× baseline, a retained 7.5% regression; four-scheduler short is 1.187×. See the [intervention](../benchmarks/language-comparison/results/actors-parity-adaptive-transfer-20260919/STATUS.md) and [baseline](../benchmarks/language-comparison/results/actors-parity-adaptive-baseline-20260919/STATUS.md) reports. This corrects the major eager-index cost without claiming universal improvement or BEAM parity.
* **Measurement tooling**: A new Rust mode in the existing actor runner compares two immutable Morrow binaries on 32×500 and 32×5,000 request/reply at one/two/four schedulers, pinned and stealing, with one warmup and five rotated measured rounds. It reuses bounded subprocess capture and independent exact-output formulas. Six runner tests pass, including descendant-pipe timeout coverage. The earlier temporary Python diagnostic capture remains outside the repository; its exact hash is retained with its raw observations.
