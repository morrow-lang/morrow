* **Status**: Adopted for task 2 of the [multi-scheduler design](../docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-19-multi-scheduler-execution-design.md); verification is tracked in ROADMAP.md
* **Decision**: Move `Session`, its identity table, `Actor` and `Supervisor` out of the collected invocation heap. Atomic reference ownership releases each record when its final owner disappears. A collected root `Exec` wrapper retains its session, each collected PID wrapper retains its actor, and each payload heap retains the actor or supervisor whose words root its payload. Actors retain their session and supervision lineage; identity-table entries, queue links and the supervisor's current actor remain borrowed pointers. This is a cycle-free ownership graph. Control mutation and callbacks remain confined to the owning invocation thread; atomic reference counts provide no permission for concurrent access.
* **Context**: Slot 0 holds ordinary program values as well as controls. A scheduler cannot independently collect it while another scheduler relies on those controls. Moving ownership outside GC also removes invocation collection's scan of foreign heap metadata. Stale PIDs must retain the exact retired actor and supervision lineage after slot reuse, without retaining its old payload. An actor without a surviving PID must still remain live until its payload heap retires.
* **Lifetime boundaries**: Control tokens release only reference ownership and accounting. Their final release never reads collected values, invokes callbacks or reenters a domain. Heap contents are destroyed before the external control words registered as roots. Scheduler completion and recovery retain temporary owners across heap retirement, and completion removes borrowed queue links before releasing storage. Controls inside any collected heap, and root ranges paired with the wrong external owner, are rejected; this supersedes Decision158's invocation-only placement rule while preserving its domain-token checks.
* **Accounting and ABI**: Native entry-point signatures and the `Exec`/PID layouts remain unchanged. The existing 64 MiB logical admission policy uses the original control-record charges, so new Rust ownership fields do not move spawn/send limits. Physical control bytes are counted once per allocation in their originating domain, independently of cloned references, and remain visible until final release. They participate in invocation collection pressure, its updated threshold and returned statistics: otherwise a tiny unreachable `Exec` wrapper could retain a large identity table without prompting collection. Actor payload collection remains local. Neither this work nor its reference counts introduce shared message payloads.
* **Compiler roots**: The generated actor entry registers its collected `Exec` wrapper before calling source `main`, then retires that root after scheduler shutdown and the final fault read. The former embedded `Exec` survived through actor-to-session GC edges; the new wrapper needs its own explicit root. An independent lowering test fails before registration is added. A compiled native test forces precise heap-0 collection before actor execution and checks that exactly the unused 8-byte closure and 32-byte PID are reclaimed while the worker still runs.
* **Evidence**: Full macOS ARM64 `cargo xtask check` passes 2,299 Rust tests across 290 suites, 317 native fixtures, 20 examples, 63 dynamic programs, 295 atomic rejections and 64+192+231 fuzz cases. The ordinary runtime suite has 120 tests, including 66,536 sequential actor transitions. Thirty-six focused memory/identity tests pass under ThreadSanitizer with no race reports; the full sanitizer suite was not rerun for this task. Seed `0x0046524e` over 5,000 actor-simulation steps matches the baseline exactly: trace `0xd4e402a412f11e2f`, 48,739 callbacks, 1,246 deliveries, 3,754 timeouts, 2,507 restarts and 10,000 churn actors, with zero live actors, messages, heap bytes and heap objects after cleanup. Independent weak-reference checks additionally prove that the final PID releases its retired actor, supervisor and session.
* **Consequences**: Task 2 is a prerequisite for independent scheduler domains, not parallel execution. Immutable published actor identities, message fragments, the session-wide concurrent budget policy, scheduler queues and OS-thread execution remain separate tasks. Precise-root coverage and preemption remain open. The independent native fixture stresses a copied stale PID through allocation pressure, supervision restart and delivery of a 16,384-byte message; runtime lifetime probes check reclamation separately from output behavior.
